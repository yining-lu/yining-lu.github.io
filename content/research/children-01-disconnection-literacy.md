Digital mental health research has increasingly examined how adolescents' digital
engagement shapes psychological well-being, yet less attention has been paid to how
adolescents actively *regulate* their connectivity and the communicative processes that
shape these practices.

Drawing on the **differential susceptibility to media effects model (DSMM)**, this study
used two-wave longitudinal data from 1,615 Chinese adolescents (*M*age = 14.93; 51.5%
female) to examine whether adolescents' digital disconnection is associated over time
with their mental health outcomes, and whether parental active mediation and digital
literacy — as family communicative and individual factors — moderated these
associations.

Results showed that adolescents' digital disconnection had **no overall association**
with subsequent mental health. Rather, its benefits were conditional on adolescents'
resources: parental active mediation marginally enhanced its association with
well-being, while digital literacy shaped both outcomes, enabling higher-literacy
adolescents to gain greater well-being benefits and experience less depression from
disconnection.

These findings extend digital mental health research by shifting attention from
adolescents' *exposure* to digital media toward their *active regulation* of
connectivity. Rather than a universal solution, digital disconnection represents a
context-dependent practice whose mental health implications depend on adolescents'
competencies and supportive communication environments.
