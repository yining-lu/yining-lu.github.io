Generative AI is no longer confined to productivity tools; it now mediates
consequential and personal decisions. This line of work follows AI into three such
settings.

The first is **misinformation correction**. In a published experiment, Dr. Shuang Chen
and I tested whether *source consistency* between misinformation and its correction
matters — that is, whether AI-generated misinformation is best countered by
AI-generated fact-checks, or whether the mismatch itself carries persuasive weight.

The second is **health communication**. Working with collaborators, I use a
mixed-methods design to examine how a health chatbot's patient-centered orientation
shapes users' continuance intention — asking whether conversational warmth genuinely
re-centers the patient or merely simulates doing so.

The third is **parenting**. Through an interview study, I am examining how parents
incorporate generative AI into early-childhood parenting: what they ask it, how much
they trust it, and how it sits alongside more traditional sources of parenting advice.
