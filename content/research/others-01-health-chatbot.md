Health chatbots offer substantial potential for improving individuals' access to
healthcare and the efficiency of health management, yet users' intention to continue
using these systems remains a major challenge. Although **patient-centered care** has
recently been widely advocated as a guiding principle for intelligent health systems,
little is known about what constitutes health chatbots' patient-centered orientation
(HC-PCO) and how it shapes user continuance intention.

Drawing on patient-centered care, the artificially intelligent device use acceptance
model, and moral dual-process theory, this study used a **mixed-methods design across
two studies** to develop and test a theoretical model.

**Study 1**, based on in-depth interviews (*N* = 31), conceptualized HC-PCO as a
hierarchical construct comprising three dimensions: *functionality* (competence,
transparency, ease of use), *humanization* (autonomy, warmth, personalization), and
*ethicality* (justice, non-maleficence).

**Study 2** (*N* = 600) tested the proposed model using survey data. HC-PCO was **not
directly associated** with continuance intention, but it was positively associated with
perceived health value and psychological closeness, and negatively associated with
perceived ethical risk. These three factors served as parallel and serial mediators
linking HC-PCO to continuance intention. Moderation analysis further showed that
perceived mind weakened the negative relationship between HC-PCO and perceived ethical
risk.

This study extends patient-centered care to the context of health human–AI interaction,
clarifies the value–risk and affective mechanisms through which HC-PCO shapes
continuance intention, and offers insights for designing health chatbots that are
valuable, ethically reassuring, and psychologically closer to users.
