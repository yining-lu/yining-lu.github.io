With the rapid development of digital media technologies, adolescents' everyday
experiences have been increasingly reshaped by a continuously connected online
environment, in which media use is deeply embedded in their social interaction,
emotional regulation, and self-development. However, existing research has
predominantly positioned adolescents as **passive recipients** of the digital
environment, focusing primarily on the negative risks of digital media use for mental
health, while paying limited attention to adolescents' active regulatory behaviors and
their potential positive developmental implications.

Drawing on **Self-Determination Theory**, this study conceptualizes voluntary digital
disconnection as an active digital practice through which adolescents intentionally
regulate their media connectivity. Using a three-wave longitudinal design, data were
collected from 1,753 adolescents in mainland China (*M*age = 14.93; 51.6% female). The
study examines the longitudinal mechanisms through which satisfaction of the three
basic psychological needs — autonomy, relatedness, and competence — link voluntary
digital disconnection to psychological resilience.

Adolescents' voluntary digital disconnection at Time 1 positively predicted
psychological resilience at Time 3, and significantly predicted increased satisfaction
of autonomy, relatedness, and competence needs at Time 2. In the parallel mediation
model, **only competence need satisfaction** significantly mediated the relationship
between digital disconnection and psychological resilience.

Overall, this study reframes adolescents from passive recipients of the digital
environment to *active regulators* of their media use, extends digital disconnection
from a mere "reduction of use" behavior to an active self-regulatory practice, and
identifies competence as a key psychological mechanism linking digital disconnection to
enhanced resilience.
